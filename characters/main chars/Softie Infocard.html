    Basic Information

    Softie is the physical representation of trauma. In his universe, he represented ‘The Deity of Rot & Trauma’. He was also frequently related to death, abandonment, abuse, betrayal, and depression or grief. Despite the harsh tones of his titles, the name ‘Softie’and his kind, sweet personality and friendly appearance is what we see today. It’s quite the contrast, and it feels uncharacteristic. In the show, we learn what all of these depressing undertones actually mean after Softie’s warmed up and has become his current, kind self. The idea of such a sweet little guy feels so foreign at first, but it’s not hard to understand. On the surface, he's a timid, but kind person. He subconsciously wears his emotions, so it’s not too difficult to tell what he’s feeling at the moment. Despite being an introverted person, he often gravitates towards his friends because he feels more confident and safe with their presence. Softie is also an extreme pacifist, but he did not get this trait from Saint’s world views, he developed this himself way before The Warehouse. This extreme pacifist world view means that he will do things that may not make sense to others at first, such as helping Farrow despite knowing he is evil.


    Personality

    In the beginning of The Warehouse, Softie thought that every action was a ploy to hurt him even more. Because of this, he often starved even more than he normally would. He was also a lot more alert and terrified of everyone, causing him to be more exhausted from the constant fight or flight. Because of this behavior, only Saint could interact with Softie for a while. He would always hide when he heard even the quietest of noises. Softie hated the amount of people around him, even if they weren't in his room. Ever so often, Saint instructed Bloom to give Softie a sketchbook, some glue and/or tape, and a few new pencils. This is because Softie liked to spend his time drawing crude sketches, and he also liked to display them on his wall. Sometimes, Saint would sneakily put a page in the fresh sketchbooks. Sometimes the page had a note written by him, stating encouraging words to comfort Softie and to let him know that nobody here is going to hurt him. These notes did little to actually comfort him, but that doesn’t mean that he didn’t look forward to seeing them. Saint would also sometimes put little drawings or photographs of him and the others. But once again, this did little to influence him, if anything, the sketchbooks and supplies being given to him comforted and made him feel safer. Softie was still extremely terrified of everyone, but he stopped flinching when someone knocked on his door to drop off food or supplies.

    Eventually, when Softie was comfortable enough, Saint would go fully inside the room, same with Bloom. Mason was also able to go inside and give Softie some jigsaw puzzles to pass the time. Softie was still extremely skittish and mute around everyone, but he was starting to warm up. He wasn’t running away and hiding when people (mainly Saint, Bloom and Mason) entered his room anymore. Softie felt less terrified of the main 3 and they were even able to address and speak to Softie directly without him feeling scared of the undertones of what they were saying.

    After a long while, Softie was finally able to receive and give physical contact with others. It was mostly just hand holding, hugging and pats, but he always had to be asked first. He still flinched, but it was progress. The first time this happened was when Softie gave Saint a hug as a thank you for patching up his face. Subconsciously, Softie had gained more confidence with his face fixed, so he was a little bit more willing to interact with people and go outside. He was still extremely skittish and terrified of new people, but he wasn’t afraid of Saint anymore. Bloom, he was still a little fearful around because she couldn’t read his mind like Saint could, and Mason… he was loud and unpredictable, he was too much for Softie.

    Softie was very hesitant to go outside with others, and if he was without his friends, he was terrified. Saint was usually with Softie when he was outside, but sometimes he would ‘be right back’ and leave Softie alone to allow him to get used to being outside without Saint.
