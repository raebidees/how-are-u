I was a fool. When I was younger, I told the government that I wouldn’t mind being drafted if it had to be done. I practically gave the government my suicide note, but I’d rather be thought of as a hero than a nobody. I wasn’t actually expecting there to be a war, nor was I hoping for one. I thought I would at least be able to tell my family goodbye, but the universe is controlled by a cruel, masochistic billionaire bully. When they knocked at my door, I was prepared to be told that soon I would have to leave, but that never happened. Instead, I was told to get on the bus. I was confused, is this the morning bus to school? Do I need a backpack? Can I at least say goodbye to my wife? The bus ride was uneventful. For hours, we drove around the city, picking up everyone. I was anxious, what was happening? I tried to calm myself using the techniques my therapist taught me. Breathe in, hold, exhale, repeat. It calmed me down somewhat, but every time the noise died down, my panic would rise. When we finally got to the base, it was pitch black outside. Everyone was put into cells, as if this was a prison. Luckily, I got to share a cell with some nice people. I hate how I can’t remember their names or their faces, especially since we were there for so long. It was an agonizing wait, but one day, we finally got taken to a different cell. All I remember in that moment was feeling tired and passing out on a bed. When I woke up, I wasn’t myself anymore. I couldn’t feel my legs, my arms were cramped, it felt like I was in a box, and my legs were chopped off. I couldn’t see, I couldn’t move- I panicked. I cried out. Then I saw light. I couldn’t recognise the person who was in front of me, they were made of metal. It was only until they spoke, I knew who it was. It was my cellmate. I cried to them, panicking about my situation. It turns out, I was one of the few picked to be the equivalent of a real life mimic. How lucky. At least I was still somewhat myself, I was still a backpack at heart. Instead of normal fabric, my limbs were metal, and my skin was like frozen denim. I was disguised as a military backpack- but my skin! My wonderful green fabric that I prided myself on so much! There aren’t many green backpacks out there, I was rare! But did being rare matter more than being in the military? At least I was given sweet metal wings to slice people with. I remember being sent out. I remember ripping innocent opponents in half. I remember my friend… I remember seeing his eyes fading. I remember running into danger to save him. I remember the pain of being shot. I remember him dying in my arms as I lost consciousness. This is the end, I thought. I’m going to die a hero. I’m going to die protecting my friend. But… I don’t remember anything about him. Not even what he looked like. My memory is cruel. After over 200 years, I woke up. Whatever they did to me practically made me immortal. I was so confused, but I didn’t have time to fully process my surroundings before the pain came. You’d think that after being asleep for 200 years all your wounds would heal, but I guess not. It made sense though, I hadn’t eaten or drank anything since. It’s not like I was in a coma in a hospital with people making sure I was still healthy. To this day, I still don’t know how I woke up. Even Saint says it’s a plot hole, but this isn’t a story, right? Sorry, that was… unnecessary. I was starving, I desperately needed something to eat, but you can’t eat if you don’t know where you are, and it was very dark. I didn’t know if it was safe to move, so I stayed as still as possible. Sorry, I should explain something. I was pretty much put in some random position, like a taxidermied animal. I was glued to stay in my pose, which sucked, because I needed to move. Through my limited vision, I took in my surroundings. I saw my reflection in some glass, which made it easier to see what was around me. I was in an exhibit, a museum exhibit. The apparel of my fallen comrades was showcased around me, with plaques that explained their significance. I tried to see my plaque, but it was upside down from my perspective. I stayed hidden for god knows how long. Every creak, every howl- even my own exhales startled me. But, even as nervous as I was, I couldn’t stay like this forever. Very carefully, I tried to move. I had no nerves in my mechanical limbs, so it wasn’t painful. It was, however, extremely annoying. Eventually, I was able to move freely. I picked off the glue that was still stuck to me, but a new problem surfaced. How do I get out of this thing? There had to be something, considering that the museum has to make sure all their stuff is properly cleaned and cared for. I looked around, I probably looked like a dog in a kennel, spinning in circles to find a way out. Finally, I found a hinge that released me. I could finally look around and find something to eat. Back when I was drafted, I was instructed to eat the fallen enemies, which was disgusting. Half of them barely had any meat on them, which was awful. Not to mention, it was extremely rude! I wouldn’t want to be the enemies’ dinner for their… cyborg mimic. That’s cruel. Because of this diet, I vowed to never eat anyone again- if I survived at least, which I did? I guess? But I was only looking for one thing: the staff room. There has to be something in there. A mini fridge, maybe even a forgotten apple. What I didn’t realize was that this museum was absolutely massive. I tried my best to avoid cameras, because I had no idea what would happen if I was found. Would I be killed? It wasn’t too difficult considering I was equipped with the perfect climbing arms and legs, so I could dangle on the ceiling and never be seen. I got lost… a lot. It was difficult to read the maps in the museum, ok? Anyway, I finally made my way to the staff room. It was locked, unsurprisingly, so I had to pick it with a random paperclip I found lying on the floor. The room was perfect- couches, a TV, vending machines, everything you would ever need. I decided to go for the vending machine, I felt bad about the idea of stealing random people’s food when there’s a vending machine right there. I looked at my options. Some snacks, candy, gross stuff… nothing looked healthy! I needed real food, not… junk! Ok, that’s annoying. I decided to get the healthiest thing in there: a peanut butter chocolate protein bar. I reached my arm in and grabbed it. What? I’m not paying, I have no money! I sat down on the couch and ate it. It tasted delicious, I couldn’t help but go for more. I was starving. By the time I was full, the vending machine was half empty. Nothing filled me up much, so I had to eat a lot. I felt terrible. Imagine how much that c-... this is a museum, they have plenty of money. What am I so sad about? As I left the room to make my way back to my exhibit, I saw someone. I jumped and hid immediately. As she got closer to my location, I got a better look of her. She was the most gorgeous woman I had ever seen. I wasn’t entirely sure what she was though. A mushroom? Lichen? Or a mix of both? I had never seen an object like her before. Times have changed a lot, apparently. She was such a gorgeous, beautiful golden yellow… I couldn’t look away. I watched her enter the staff room with caution, as if checking to see if anyone was watching. Of course, I was watching, but she didn’t know. I don’t know why, but I couldn’t resist getting closer. As the door closed behind her, I slinked out of the shadows and leaned against the door to at least hear her. At first, there was no noise, but then I heard something that broke my heart. She started to cry. I wanted to see what was wrong, but I couldn’t. I heard her cry for a few minutes before her sniffles faded into silence. That was my que to leave. I couldn’t just leave her there, I had to know why she was crying, so instead of going back to my exhibit, I hid above again. She left the room soon after she stopped crying. I quietly followed her as she made her rounds, making sure that everything was in check. Does that count as stalking? I watched as she stopped every once in a while, speaking to the random exhibits. She talked about her past, as if the random trinkets could listen and relate. I quickly realized that she was getting dangerously close to where I should have been, so I crawled over there as quietly yet as quickly as I could. She stopped in front of me, and my heart started to race. Did she know?? But she started to speak to me, the same as she had done to the others. “You’ve always amazed me. The fact that you were… real. Why? But most importantly, how? I can’t imagine being in your shoes. The war exhibits always freak me out, but they also make me thankful for my life. People like you, Byron. You’re like… a hero, or-” She was cut off by someone. I tried to stay still, but I couldn’t help but flinch as the new figure loudly barked at the kind staff lady. This new person was a pinkish white marble statue of a fox. His posture was defensive as he scolded the staff member for slacking and doing a bad job. Through this conversation, I learned that the beautiful lady was named Delta, and the rude marble fox was named Verin. Out of everything, one single thing stuck out to me the most. Delta had given me a name, Byron. It fit me in a way, it felt robotic. I couldn’t remember my original name, so this new name stuck like glue. Byron was my new identity. Verin gave me terrible energy, who was this man? How rude of him to make Delta cry. Once they were mostly out of earshot, I got out and followed behind them. Verin was yelling at Delta every time she stopped to stare, telling her that this behavior will get her fired. I flinched every time he barked. Of course, my flinching rattled my claws against the pipes on the ceiling. This proved to be a problem, as every time I flinched, they looked my way. I had to hide in the shade to make sure they didn’t see me. After god knows how long, the sun started to rise. Verin left Delta alone as he prepared all the things for visitors and day staff. I went back to my exhibit for the day. It was very interesting to see the reactions of citizens. Some were amazed, others grimaced. The little kids and the engineers were the most interested in me. It felt good to be seen, it felt like I was a celebrity. Yes! Come gawk at my unique anatomy and past, I am certainly a sight to see! Sometimes, when the parents looked away, I would move in front of the kids. I would blink, or shift a limb, and sometimes I would directly try to scare the kid if they pissed me off. It was fun, I didn’t feel lonely. But occasionally, someone would yell or scream or drop a particularly loud metal water bottle on the floor and I would flinch. Not many noticed my reactions, but the ones who did accused their own head. The days and nights were simple and predictable. In the day, I stayed put and occasionally spooked parents and kids. The nights, I would follow Delta and eat hidden food within the various staff only rooms. That was until I started paying more attention to Delta. She talked about how she was struggling to pay her bills, so I would… conveniently find money and conveniently place it nearby her with a scribbled note about how it's totally not from me. I tried my best to help her. Time management? Secretly change all the clock’s hands. That was my life for the next few months. I thought this day would be normal. The day was fine, but as the hours stretched on, people stopped coming in, and the sun went to rest. I was hungry, it was tiring work to stay still all day! Once the lights turned off, I left. I was careful, until I saw Delta. She was sitting alone, crying into her palms. I needed to know what was bothering her, but I couldn’t appear, what would happen to me? Instead, I made a rattling noise. She perked up, looking confused. I bolted to my exhibit to make even more noise, hoping she would follow. She took the bait, as expected. Surely enough, I saw her walk over to my exhibit. I wasn’t surprised when she walked past to investigate where the noise might’ve come from, I would have done the same. Then, she looked my way. Finally, she was looking at me. “You don’t mind if I talk to you, right? You’re the only thing I trust not to tell on me… I know it’s stupid. I’m talking to a dead robot- or whatever you are. But I feel like you would understand. If only you were alive.” This is horrible! I can’t be seen, but Delta… she wanted to see me! This was a tough decision… what to do, what to do? I was scared of the thought, but I acted on impulse. For the first time in god knows how long, I spoke to her in the quietest voice I could muster. “What would you do if I was alive?” It was odd, her lack of reaction. It seemed as if she just imagined I spoke, or was I not loud enough? “...I would, I don’t know. Hug you? No… that’s weird. I can’t… I would… keep you a secret. That’s for sure.” She would keep me a secret? It was too good to be true! I couldn’t control my excitement, I had to move, or do something! I slowly shifted my very uncomfortable posture to look her in the eyes. She was extremely surprised to see me move. “Wait- what?!” I flinched. Did I do something wrong? I froze, what if I made the wrong move? Was I toast? “Y-y-you… You’re alive?! All this time- and I never knew! I… I had a feeling there was something different about you-!” “I… I have only been awake for a day. Is it ok for me to get out?” “Yes, please come out! I want to see you up close…” I got out and stood in front of her, before kneeling down to her level to get a better look at her features. “You are… Delta, correct?” “Y-yes! I’m Delta… you are…?” “You call me Byron, right? Why not keep the name.” “Ok-! Byron…” “I have a question. What… are you?” “Me? I’m the tail of an Everburn Tangle…” “Oh..? What is… an Everburn Tangle?” Our wonderful conversation was cut short by Verin yelling across the hall. I flinched, Verin was danger. Delta was in danger. My instincts told me to attack, so I did. In a split second, I grabbed Verin and flung him across the room. Delta gasped and took a step closer to me. Verin growled and hissed in pain. He hadn’t registered what was going on yet, but when he did, he was shocked and terrified. Imagine if a war veteran saw you as a threat, I would be terrified too! Verin called the police right there in front of us. Delta screamed at Verin and told him to stop and end the call, but it was too late. I told Delta to stay back. I was terrified of losing the only thing that actually cared about me. Would I be killed? Taken away? Handcuffed and sent to jail? I lunged at Verin, knocking the phone out of his hands as he hit the floor. His marble fur cracked under the impact, but I was too terrified to notice or even care. My mind was solely focussed on making sure nobody came for me. I sliced his body with my wings, and I pounced him into the ground like I was doing some intense form of CPR. His nose bled, and his cracks pulsed with blood as I continued to drive him into the floor. When I saw Verin pass out, I stopped my advance. The adrenaline faded into panic and horror, I was worried I scared Delta, or worse, killed Verin. I was surely dead now. I turned to Delta, bracing myself for her to be terrified of me. I saw her eyes, she was crying again. I rushed over to her and kneeled down to her level. She took a few steps back, I wasn’t surprised, but it still hurt me. I apologized profusely, but she continued to back away from me. I stopped trying to hold her, I knew this wasn’t the time. The silence was deafening, apart from my heart throbbing in my ears and the occasional drop of blood from Verin. The silence broke when we heard footsteps. To my horror, three people showed up, but their uniforms were nowhere to be seen. All three of them were backpacks, just like me, down to the wings. I was confused, but my confusion quickly turned to panic as one of them pointed at me and shouted “That’s our guy! Get em!” I made a startled battle cry that was not threatening at all, and two of them ran up to me. The one that stayed behind looked like a backpack trying to be a medkit, and she was not having anything. All she did was sigh and look at her nails. The other two tried to grab me, but I pushed them away with little to no effort. One of the backpacks looked like an FBI agent with a shotgun, and the other just looked like Pyro from Team Fortress Two. While I was trying to knock off the ankle biters, the medkit backpack went over to Verin and bandaged him up. A part of me was pissed that they were helping the villain, but another part of me realized that they were doing the right thing, and that they had no idea who was the good or bad guy in this situation. As I observed this, I hesitated for just one second. One second was all they needed. They had grabbed me and pinned me down. I couldn’t move. I heard the medkit say something along the lines of “Oh, I guess that’s done.” I was a little hurt hearing that, were they expecting more of me? They came up to me and looked at me with that same annoyed expression, as if I was an inconvenience. I wasn’t worried about my position, I was however worried about Delta. I looked for her in the corners of my eyes, but she had gone. She had left me. That hurt me the most, the fact that she had run away. I don’t know what happened, or how it happened, for my vision went white. I didn’t move. I didn’t want to. I don’t know why. I was dragged across the void until we reached some sort of warehouse. I was placed on a couch, and I got a good view of the place. Everyone else was very similar to me in there, just like the people who caught me. There were very few differences between everyone. Some were fluffy, some were different colors, some were loud, some were reserved, and one was… Saint. I mentioned Saint earlier. He’s the owner of the place. He knows way too much to not look like a villain, but he’s not. He’s one of the nicest guys I’ve ever met. He told me what happened to Delta after I was taken. Delta had run away to hide from the situation, not knowing that I would disappear. Verin, he had lived, thanks to the medkit. Verin was fired, and Delta got promoted. Delta lived way happier after Verin was off of her. Verin got a new job, working as a glassblower, since that was his hobby. Now, here I am. Sometimes, I must take care of Softie. He is a sweet little boy, though he has a large patch of fabric across half of his face. Saint told me it was because of an injury that never got to heal. I’m happy now, happier than I was before. I enjoy my time here.
